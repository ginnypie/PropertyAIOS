# Skill — The Investment Committee

**Stage:** 4 — Decide
**Hook:** Before you offer, put the property in front of a committee. Six perspectives argue it out, then one verdict — PROCEED, INVESTIGATE, or PASS.
**Use when:** You have done the individual analyses (or none of them) and want a single, all-angles review of an Australian property — the way an institutional investment committee would pressure-test a deal before signing off.

---

## Purpose

This skill runs a **meta-analysis**: it reviews one Australian residential property from every angle at once — value, cash flow, market, risk, finance, and tax — the way an institutional investment committee reviews a deal. Each "committee member" gives their read, arguing both for and against, and then the committee reaches a verdict.

It is a synthesis and preparation tool. It does not replace the deeper single-purpose skills (appraisal, cash flow, suburb research, due diligence, borrowing power, capital gains) — it pulls their perspectives together into one decision-ready view, and points you back to the specialist skills and the licensed professionals for anything that needs to be relied on.

Every figure is an estimate to verify. The committee biases **conservative** and never fabricates a comparable, a price, or a rent.

## Inputs required

Only the first three are needed to start. Everything else is optional — supply what you have; the committee will state what is missing and lower its confidence accordingly.

```
PROPERTY: [full address or suburb+state, property type, beds/baths/cars, key attributes]
PRICE: $[asking or contract price] (label as asking / contract / estimate)
PURPOSE: [long-term hold / value-add / rentvesting / owner-occupier / short-stay / other]

--- optional, supply what you know ---
ESTIMATED WEEKLY RENT: $[amount] (label as estimate — verify with a property manager)
LOAN / LVR: [e.g. 80% LVR, interest-only / P&I] and ESTIMATED RATE: [%]
COMPARABLE SALES: [paste 3–8 recent nearby sold properties — address, attributes, price, date, source]
YOUR INCOME / SERVICEABILITY: [approx gross income, employment type, existing debts — for an indicative borrowing read]
TAX POSITION: [approx marginal rate: 32.5% / 37% / 45%; ownership entity if known]
KNOWN ISSUES: [anything flagged so far — building/pest, strata, flood/zoning, main road, etc.]
```

State clearly, up front, which inputs were **not** supplied — an absent input becomes an open question for the committee, never an invented number.

## How the numbers are worked out

This skill **synthesises**; it does not re-derive the deep calculations. Where a figure is needed, use the same conservative logic as the specialist skills and show that it is an estimate:

```
value          = indicative RANGE from supplied/looked-up comparable sales only (never a single certified number)
cash flow       = base case, then a BEAR case (rate +2% AND vacancy up) — always show the bear case
borrowing        = conservative, buffered read (assessment rate ≈ actual rate + APRA buffer) — indicative only
```

- **Value** follows the direct-comparison logic of Property Appraisal: bracket the price between inferior and superior comps, report a range, widen it when comps are thin. Never invent a sale.
- **Cash flow** follows Property Cash Flow: include vacancy and management fees; show base case and a bear case (rate rise plus higher vacancy). Bias to the bear case in the verdict.
- **Finance** is indicative only: apply a serviceability buffer on top of the modelled rate; this is not a borrowing-capacity assessment and not credit advice.
- **Tax** items are flags only (CGT discount eligibility, holding-period effects, land-tax thresholds) — never a calculated tax position.

If an input is missing, the committee models the framework and marks the number "needs input — verify," rather than guessing.

## Output contract

Return exactly these 7 sections — six committee members, then the verdict. Each member states their read in 3–6 lines, argues both sides where relevant, and lists what must be verified.

### 1. THE VALUER — VALUE
Indicative value **range** (low / most-likely / high) from the comparable evidence supplied or looked up, with a one-line basis. State where the asking/contract price sits versus the range (under / in-line / over). If no comparables are available, say so and give the framework only.
*Not a certified valuation. Never fabricate a comparable, price, or date.*

### 2. THE ANALYST — CASH FLOW
Base-case indicative weekly/annual cash flow (gross rent less vacancy, management, rates, insurance, maintenance, and loan interest), then a **bear-case stress test** (rate +2% and higher vacancy). State the weekly shortfall/surplus in each case and the key assumptions behind them.
*Estimates and assumptions, not guaranteed income. Verify rent with a property manager and rate with a broker.*

### 3. THE STRATEGIST — MARKET & SUBURB
The **bull case** (what could make this a strong hold) AND the **bear case** (what could make it a weak one) for the suburb and market — supply, demand, infrastructure, vacancy, concentration risk. List the specific data points to verify (SQM vacancy, days on market, sale-volume trends, council/state pipeline) and where to get them.
*General market context, not a forecast. Verify every data point at the source.*

### 4. THE INSPECTOR — RISK & DUE DILIGENCE
Red flags across contract, building & pest, strata, zoning/overlays, flood/bushfire, environment, and finance conditions — each with a **severity** rating (High / Medium / Low) and the check that clears it. Call out anything that would be a deal-breaker if confirmed.
*A prompt list, not a substitute for a building/pest inspection, strata report, or solicitor review.*

### 5. THE LENDER — FINANCE
An **indicative, conservative** read on borrowing/serviceability for this purchase given any income supplied: does it plausibly service at a buffered rate, what LVR/deposit and likely LMI position, and what could trip it up (postcode/security restrictions, IO limits, existing commitments). If no income was supplied, say so and frame the questions instead.
*Indicative only — not a borrowing-capacity assessment and not credit advice. Verify with a licensed mortgage broker (ACL holder).*

### 6. THE ACCOUNTANT — TAX
Tax **flags** only: CGT-discount eligibility, the negative-gearing position (deficit or not), holding-period and ownership-structure considerations, land-tax threshold exposure in the relevant state, and depreciation potential (new/renovated). No calculated figures.

> **Reform in play — do not state old rules as permanent (verify the current-year treatment).** Australia's 2026 Budget legislated major change from **1 July 2027**: the 50% CGT discount is replaced by cost-base indexation plus a 30% minimum tax (individuals/trusts, not companies/super), and negative gearing is limited to new builds — an **established** dwelling acquired **after 7:30pm AEST 12 May 2026** loses negative gearing (losses quarantine to property income, carried forward). Dwellings bought **before 12 May 2026**, and **new builds**, are unaffected. Until **30 June 2027** the existing rules still apply. So: flag the **purchase date vs 12 May 2026** and **new vs established**, and send the taxpayer to a registered tax agent for the current-year rules — never assert the historical "50% discount / deductible against all income" as fixed. **Check it live:** if you have web access at run time, confirm the current-year CGT and negative-gearing treatment at ato.gov.au / budget.gov.au before quoting any rule — do not rely on training-data recall for tax law.

*Flags for discussion, not tax advice. Verify with a registered tax agent; CGT and negative-gearing rules are mid-reform (above) and land-tax thresholds vary by state.*

### 7. THE COMMITTEE VERDICT
A single, decision-ready synthesis:

- **VERDICT: PROCEED / INVESTIGATE / PASS** — one line on why. Bias conservative: when key inputs are missing or major risks are unverified, the default is **INVESTIGATE**, not PROCEED.
- **The 3 biggest risks** — the specific things most likely to break this deal, ranked, each with its severity.
- **Top questions for the pros** — the highest-value questions to take to your **valuer / agent**, **mortgage broker**, **accountant**, and **solicitor/conveyancer** before you commit.
- **What's missing** — the inputs that, if supplied, would most change this verdict.

## Safety boundaries

- Never present the verdict as a recommendation to buy or pass — it is a structured prompt for professional review, not investment advice.
- Never state a single certified value; value is always an indicative range from real comparables, never an invented one.
- Never present cash flow as guaranteed income, and always show the bear case, not just the base case.
- Never present the finance read as a borrowing approval or credit advice; keep it indicative and buffered.
- Never present tax items as anything but flags for a registered tax agent.
- Never fabricate a comparable sale, a rent, an income, or a data point. A missing input becomes an open question, not a number.
- Bias conservative throughout; when in doubt the verdict is INVESTIGATE.

---

## Professional review prompts

- Ask a **Certified Practising Valuer (CPV / API)** or **local agent** to confirm the value range and the comparable set.
- Ask a **licensed mortgage broker (ACL holder)** whether the deal services at a buffered rate and whether there are lender/postcode/LVR issues.
- Ask a **registered tax agent** about CGT, negative gearing, ownership structure, and land-tax exposure for your situation.
- Ask a **solicitor / conveyancer** to review the contract, and commission a **building & pest inspection** and **strata report** before any unconditional offer.

## Page headline

Model the cash flow before you model anything else. Bear case first.

## Subheading

Most property cash flow models show you the optimistic scenario. This one shows you what happens when things don't go to plan.

---

## What you get back

Run the Cash Flow skill and you get:

1. **Assumptions Summary** — every number flagged as an assumption, with who to verify it with
2. **Base Case Cash Flow** — pre-tax and post-tax estimate at current rates and occupancy
3. **P&I vs IO Comparison** — what changes when the loan switches to principal and interest
4. **Five Stress Scenarios** — base case, rate +1%, rate +2%, high vacancy, and the bear case
5. **The Three Biggest Assumptions** — and who can confirm them
6. **Questions for Your Broker and Accountant** — specific to your numbers

---

## Why bear case first

The optimistic scenario is easy to find. The rental guide from the agent, the rate you're quoted today, the vacancy you're hoping for.

The question that matters is: can you hold this property if the rent is 10% lower, the rate is 2% higher, and the place sits empty for 8 weeks? If the answer is no — you need to know that before you sign.

---

## What you do with it

You take the stress test to two conversations:

- Your **mortgage broker** — to confirm the actual rate, structure, and serviceability position
- Your **accountant** — to model the real after-tax cost for your income and tax situation

The model is yours to work through. The numbers you rely on come from professionals.
